Changing in Denver

Burnham Yard: a stadium plan with parks and a trail

The Broncos’ updated Burnham Yard concepts go beyond the stadium. The August renderings show public green spaces, a proposed walking and biking trail called the Mile High Line, and a new use for the historic locomotive shop. The plans also call for reconnecting streets through the former rail yard and directing event traffic north and south. These are preliminary concepts that will continue to change through public input and coordination with the city and other agencies. The renderings show what is proposed, not places you can visit yet.

Explore the preliminary Burnham Yard plans
 

Your Colfax bus stop may have moved to the middle

Routes 15 and 15L began using the new center bus-only lanes between Broadway and Colorado Boulevard on September 27. Riders now board and exit at stations in the middle of Colfax along that segment. That is a change to the existing routes as construction is completed there, so check your stop before heading out. RTD’s September changes also introduced hourly SkyRide Route AL between Longmont and Denver International Airport, with stops at the Niwot and Lafayette Park-n-Rides. The same service update lists other bus and rail schedule changes across the system.

Check RTD’s route and boarding changes

Deep-pick events

Glow at the Gardens

October 15–18 & 21–24 · 5:30–9 p.m.
Denver Botanic Gardens, York Street · Denver

The Scene
The York Street gardens become an evening walking route lined with real, locally grown pumpkins, hand-carved sculptures and live performers. This year there are 3 one-way paths. Take all 3 if you want to see the full display.

Worth Knowing
Buy a ticket for your date and entry window before going. There are no tickets at the door. Nonmember admission is $37 for adults and $29 for children ages 3–15; children 2 and younger enter free.

16 LIVE: ¡Dale, Disco!

Thursday, October 15 · 5–9 p.m.
Glenarm Plaza, 1600 Glenarm Place · Denver

The Scene
The final 16 LIVE of the year puts reggaeton and disco together downtown, with DJ sets, dance instruction and a silent-disco battle beneath the Quaking Gold art installation. The amplified set starts at 5 p.m., followed by dancing at 6 and the silent disco at 7.

Worth Knowing
RSVP through the organizer’s page if you want a silent-disco headset. Supplies are limited. The plaza can be entered from either the 16th Street or 17th Street side.

Westy Fest

Saturday, October 17 · Noon–8 p.m.
Westminster City Park, 10455 N. Sheridan Blvd. · Westminster

The Scene
Carnival games, live music, trick-or-treating and a pumpkin patch fill City Park. The balloons stay on the ground, with inflation planned around 5:45 p.m. A drone show is scheduled for about 7:45 p.m.; both depend on the weather.

Worth Knowing
Admission and the listed festival activities are free. Food, beer, crafts and pumpkins cost extra. Parking is limited, so use the city’s shuttles from College Hill Library, City Hall or Westminster High School. They run from noon until 9 p.m.

Harvest Haunt Express

October 17–18 · Activities 10 a.m.–3 p.m.
Colorado Railroad Museum, 17155 W. 44th Ave. · Golden

The Scene
A coal-burning steam locomotive pulls the museum’s Halloween train rides. Between departures, explore the hay-bale maze, silly graveyard and not-so-spooky railcars. Costumes are encouraged for trick-or-treating, and the grounds stay open from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m.

Worth Knowing
Online admission with a train ride is $17 for adults and $7 for ages 2–17. Advance purchase saves $5 per ticket compared with onsite pricing. Capacity is limited; arrive 30 minutes before your booked departure.

Book a Harvest Haunt train ride
 

DIVA at the Denver Art Museum

On view throughout October · Through January 31, 2027
Denver Art Museum, Hamilton Building, Level 2 · Denver

The Scene
Stage costumes, photographs and performance history trace nearly 100 artists, from Josephine Baker and Maria Callas to Cher, Prince and Rihanna. Denver is the exhibition’s only U.S. stop. About 250 objects show how performers have used what they wear to shape their public identities.

Worth Knowing
DIVA requires an exhibition ticket, with a discount for museum members. Choose a visit date through the museum’s ticket page and check its visit guide for current access details. This is a separate exhibition ticket, not simply a general-admission visit.

Plan a DIVA visit and buy tickets

Local Spotlight

Milpero: dinner built around corn

Milpero earned its first Michelin star in September, but the interesting part is what chef Johnny Curiel is doing with corn. At 3455 Ringsby Court in the TAXI campus, the restaurant serves an 18-course Mexican tasting menu built around masa made in-house and heirloom corn grown southeast of Boulder with the Masa Seed Foundation. Dinner moves through different spaces: a welcome room, a cold counter, a wine and fermentation room, then a hot counter. There are 16 seats, with 8 guests beginning the experience at a time. This is a planned evening out: allow about 3 hours, and expect $225 per person for dinner, with beverage pairings extra. Reservations are required, and there are no walk-ins. New reservations open on the first of each month at 8 a.m. for the following month. Review the cancellation terms before booking, and contact the restaurant at least a week ahead about allergies or dietary restrictions.

Neighborhood Spotlight

West Highland: a walk along West 32nd

Start around West 32nd Avenue and Lowell Boulevard, where Highland Square puts shops, restaurants and places to stop for a drink along the same stretch. Walk a few blocks before choosing where to eat, rather than making the whole afternoon about one reservation. Fire on the Mountain is at 3801 West 32nd, with wings, a full bar, table service and a patio. Sweet Cow is a little farther east at 3475 West 32nd, so you can finish with a scoop and keep walking. Its flavors change, and the shop posts the current selection online. If you want a longer walk, continue toward Highland Park on the neighborhood’s eastern edge. Keep the route flexible: browse the storefronts, stop somewhere that looks interesting and leave yourself time to sit down. You do not need to build a packed itinerary to spend an afternoon here. Check individual business hours before you go, especially if you have one particular stop in mind.
